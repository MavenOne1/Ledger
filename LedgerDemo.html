Start here

Three things get you running. Everything else is optional.

  1. On Today, type the balance your bank shows right now and tap Update.
  2. On Plan, add your pay under Money coming in, and your bills under Expenses.
  3. On Activity, log things as you spend them.

That's it. The forecast builds itself from there.

The three numbers on Today

Bank says — the last balance you typed in. It only changes when you update it.

Not yet cleared — things you've logged that haven't hit your account yet. Card charges take days to post, and cash never posts at all.

Available — the first minus the second. This is your real money, and it's the number to trust. Your bank will usually show more than this, because your bank doesn't know about the coffee you bought an hour ago.

Free to spend before payday is different again. It's the lowest your balance gets before more money arrives, so it's what you can spend today without coming up short later.

Logging what you spend

Tap Activity → Add an expense. Name it, put in the amount, pick the day it happened, and choose a category.

Leave the tick box empty if it hasn't shown up at your bank yet — which is almost always the case right after you spend. It comes straight off Available so you don't accidentally spend the same money twice.

You can date it in the past. Bought lunch with cash on Tuesday and forgot? Log it Thursday, dated Tuesday. It still comes off your balance, because your bank never saw it.

When it finally appears on your statement, go to Not yet at the bank and tap the green tick. It moves to history and your balance doesn't change — it was already counted.

Things that start and stop

This is what the app is really for. Every bill and every income can have a Starts on and an Ends on date. You never have to remember to come back and change something — set it once and the forecast handles the switch on its own.

You're getting a raise in October

Don't edit your current pay. Instead:

1. Open your existing pay and set Ends on to your last cheque at the old rate.

2. Add a new income at the new amount with Starts on set to the first cheque at the new rate.

Your forecast now steps up on exactly the right day, and October onward reflects the raise while September still reflects reality.

Paying double on a credit card for six months

1. Open the card payment and set Ends on to the last normal payment.

2. Add a second bill at the doubled amount, Starts on the following month, Ends on six months later.

3. Add a third at the normal amount starting after that, if it carries on.

Now you can see exactly what those six lean months look like before you commit to them — and whether you can actually afford it.

Rent is going up

Same shape. End the old rent on its last payment, add the new amount starting the month it kicks in. Both sit in your list so you can see the handover.

A loan that finishes

Just set Ends on to the final payment date. It disappears from the forecast after that, and you'll see your breathing room widen on the day it clears.

A one-off you know about — insurance, a flight, Christmas

Add it under Expenses and choose One time, then pick the date. It shows up once in the forecast and tells you what your balance looks like on the other side of it.

How often each option means

  • Every day — food, coffee, cigarettes. Small things that add up.
  • Every week — pick the weekday.
  • Every 2 weeks — pick the next date it lands, and it counts forward from there.
  • Twice a month — two days of your choosing, like the 1st and 15th. Rent split in two.
  • Every month — one day of the month.
  • Every 2 months — for bills like water that only come every other month.
  • One time — a single dated event.

If you pick a day that doesn't exist in a short month — the 31st in February — it falls on the last day of that month instead.

Reading the forecast

Every day for the next 60 days, with what's due and your balance after it.

  • A green edge means money arrives that day.
  • A red row means you'd go below zero. That's a warning you can act on weeks early.

If something you've already logged matches a scheduled bill on the same day, the app skips the scheduled one so it isn't counted twice.

Keep a backup

Your data lives on this device and nowhere else. That's the privacy promise — but it means there's no copy to fall back on if the device is lost or the browser data is cleared.

Go to Privacy → Save a backup file every few weeks. Save it to iCloud Drive or anywhere you keep files. Restoring takes one tap.
